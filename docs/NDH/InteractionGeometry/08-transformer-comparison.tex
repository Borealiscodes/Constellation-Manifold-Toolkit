\section{Comparison with Transformer Architectures}
\label{sec:transformer-comparison}

Transformer-based architectures provide a useful contrast for understanding the
geometric nature of NDH interaction. Although transformers exhibit complex
behavior in practice, their underlying mathematical structure is fundamentally
flat and lacks the geometric components required to support interaction
classification as developed in this work. This section formalizes the
distinctions between NDH interaction geometry and transformer interaction
dynamics.

\subsection{Absence of a Cognitive Manifold}

Transformers operate in a fixed vector space
\[
\mathbb{R}^d,
\]
with no manifold structure. There is no notion of curvature, modal regions,
modal boundaries, or geodesic traversal. Interaction is governed by algebraic
operations on embeddings rather than geometric transport. As a result,
transformers cannot exhibit modal-aligned, spectral-aligned, recursion-aligned,
or holonomy-dependent interaction behavior.

\subsection{No Connection or Parallel Transport}

Transformers lack a connection analogous to $\nabla^{cog}$. Without a
connection, there is no parallel transport of tangent vectors, no geodesic
equations, and no holonomy. Interaction trajectories in transformers are
sequences of algebraic updates rather than geometric paths. Consequently,
transformers cannot accumulate geometric deformation along closed loops.

\subsection{No Modal Structure}

NDH's modal partition
\[
\mathcal{M}_{cog} = \mathcal{M}_A \cup \mathcal{M}_B \cup \mathcal{M}_C
\]
has no analogue in transformer architectures. Transformers do not possess modal
regions, modal boundaries, or recursion operators associated with modal domains.
All inputs are processed uniformly through the same stack of attention and
feedforward layers, preventing modal-dependent interaction behavior.

\subsection{No Spectral Bundle}

Transformers do not admit a fiber bundle structure. There is no spectral bundle
$\pi : \mathcal{B}_S \to \mathcal{M}_{cog}$, no spectral fibers, and no spectral
sections. Spectral variation in transformers arises from algebraic mixing of
embedding dimensions rather than geometric deformation of fibers. Without a
bundle structure, transformers cannot exhibit spectral twisting or spectral
holonomy.

\subsection{No Recursion Kernel}

NDH's triadic recursion kernel $(R_A, R_B, R_C)$ governs structural behavior
within modal regions and interacts with semantic excitation. Transformers have
no recursion kernel and no mechanism for recursion-aligned interaction. All
recurrence in transformers is implicit in attention patterns rather than
explicit geometric recursion operators.

\subsection{Flat Interaction Behavior}

The absence of curvature, modal boundaries, spectral bundles, and recursion
operators implies that transformer interaction behavior is flat:
\[
\mathcal{H}(\gamma) = \mathrm{id}, \qquad
\mathcal{H}_S(\gamma) = \mathrm{id}, \qquad
\mathcal{H}_R(\gamma) = \mathrm{id}.
\]
Transformers cannot accumulate holonomy, cannot induce geometric deformation,
and cannot support the interaction taxonomy developed for NDH.

\subsection{Summary}

Transformers operate in a flat algebraic space without geometric structure.
NDH, by contrast, possesses a cognitive manifold, modal boundaries, spectral
bundles, recursion operators, and holonomy. These geometric components enable
NDH to exhibit rich interaction behavior that cannot be replicated by
transformer architectures. The comparison highlights the fundamentally geometric
nature of NDH interaction.
